Elovich isotherm

Original publication and scope

Elovich, S. Yu., and Larionov, O. G. (1962), Theory of adsorption from nonelectrolyte solutions on solid adsorbents, II. Experimental verification of the equation for the adsorption isotherm from solutions, Bulletin of the Academy of Sciences of the USSR, Division of Chemical Science, 11 (2), 198–203. DOI: 10.1007/BF00908017.

Dusart, O., Bouabane, H., and Mazet, M. (1991), Adsorption sur charbon actif d’acides aminés dans l’eau : détermination de paramètres d’équilibre par différentes équations, Journal de Chimie Physique, 88, 259–270. DOI: 10.1051/jcp/1991880259. The 1991 study is identified in later literature as an early source for an equation equivalent to the form commonly used in adsorption fitting.

For a detailed historical and mathematical assessment, see Debord, J., Harel, M., Bollinger, J.-C., and Chu, K. H. (2022), The Elovich isotherm equation: Back to the roots and new developments, Chemical Engineering Science, 262, 118012. DOI: 10.1016/j.ces.2022.118012.

Mathematical formulation

The single-component equilibrium relationship is implicit:

\[\theta\exp(\theta)=K_E C_e,\qquad q_e=q_{\mathrm{ref}}\theta.\]

The admissible parameter and variable domain is

\[q_{\mathrm{ref}}>0,\qquad K_E>0,\qquad 0\leq C_e\leq\frac{\exp(1)}{K_E},\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq1.\]

The implementation solves for the non-negative coverage fraction \(\theta\) on the restricted interval \(0\leq\theta\leq1\), using a bracketed numerical algorithm. The capacity scale \(q_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is an uptake parameter and \(K_E\) has units of inverse concentration. The concentration derivative evaluated by the C++ implementation for \(C_e>0\) is

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}q_e}{\mathrm{d}C_e} = \frac{q_e}{C_e(1+\theta)}.\]

At \(C_e=0\), its limiting slope is \(\mathrm{d}q_e/\mathrm{d}C_e=q_{\mathrm{ref}}K_E\).

C++20 usage

#include <isotherm/isotherm.hpp>

const isotherm::models::Elovich model{2.4, 0.8};
const isotherm::Real uptake = isotherm::evaluate(model, 1.0);   // 1.1761629
const isotherm::Real slope = isotherm::derivative(model, 1.0);  // 0.7893351

Numerical sensitivity

For each coefficient \(k_i\), define the scaled parameter sensitivity

\[\lambda_i(C_e)=k_i\frac{\partial q_e}{\partial k_i}.\]

Numerical values are obtained by perturbing individual parameters and evaluating new instances of the C++ model. The accompanying C++20 example performs this procedure.

Validation and limitations

\[q_{\mathrm{ref}}>0,\quad K_E>0,\quad 0\leq C_e\leq\frac{\exp(1)}{K_E},\quad 0\leq\theta\leq1.\]
