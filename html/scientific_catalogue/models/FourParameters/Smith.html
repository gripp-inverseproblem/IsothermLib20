Smith isotherm

Original publication and scope

Smith, S. E. (1947), The Sorption of Water Vapor by High Polymers, Journal of the American Chemical Society, 69 (3), 646–651. DOI: 10.1021/ja01195a053.

Smith’s original equation (6) describes a two-coefficient moisture-sorption relation at a fixed temperature. The modified three-coefficient expression below allows its intercept to depend linearly on absolute temperature. A temperature-dependent Smith relationship has been reported, for example, in the study of moisture sorption in soybean grains. The temperature term is not part of Smith’s 1947 equation.

The C++ class stores three empirical coefficients and a fixed water activity for its temperature section. Its four stored parameters do not represent four independently fitted material coefficients.

Mathematical formulation

\[q_e(T;A,B,C,a_w)=(A+BT)-C\ln(1-a_w).\]

The admissible parameter and input domain is

\[A>0,\quad B\in\mathbb{R},\quad C>0,\quad 0\leq a_w<1,\quad T>0\;\mathrm{K},\quad A+BT>0.\]

Here \(a_w=p_w/p_w^{\mathrm{sat}}\) is dimensionless water activity, where the vapour pressure and saturation pressure refer to the same temperature. \(T\) is the absolute temperature in kelvin. \(A\) and \(C\) have units of equilibrium moisture content, while \(B\) has units of equilibrium moisture content per kelvin. Literature expressions written as \(q_e=a-bt_{\mathrm{C}}-c\ln(1-a_w)\), with \(t_{\mathrm{C}}\) in degrees Celsius, correspond to the kelvin-based form through \(A=a+273.15b\), \(B=-b\), \(C=c\).

The temperature derivative at fixed stored parameters is

\[\frac{\partial q_e}{\partial T}=B.\]

The derivative with respect to stored water activity is distinct:

\[\frac{\partial q_e}{\partial a_w}=\frac{C}{1-a_w}.\]

The C++ derivative(model, T) function returns \(B\), not the water-activity derivative. The latter can be calculated by comparing new instances constructed at different activities.

C++20 usage

#include <isotherm/isotherm.hpp>

const isotherm::models::Smith model{1.4, -0.001, 0.55, 0.5}; // A, B, C, a_w
constexpr isotherm::Real temperature_k = 300.0;              // K
const isotherm::Real uptake = isotherm::evaluate(model, temperature_k);   // 1.4812309
const isotherm::Real slope = isotherm::derivative(model, temperature_k);  // -0.001

Numerical sensitivity

For each model parameter \(k_i\), the scaled sensitivity is

\[\lambda_i(T)=k_i\frac{\partial q_e(T)}{\partial k_i}.\]

Numerical values are obtained by perturbing individual parameters and evaluating new instances of the C++ model. The accompanying C++20 example performs this procedure.

Validation and limitations

\[A>0,\quad B\in\mathbb{R},\quad C>0,\quad 0\leq a_w<1,\quad T>0\;\mathrm{K},\quad A+BT>0.\]

At a single fixed water activity, varying temperature identifies the slope \(B\) and only the combined intercept \(A-C\ln(1-a_w)\). Determining \(A\) and \(C\) separately therefore requires additional activity conditions or independent information. The original Smith relation was developed for a restricted moisture-sorption regime; mathematical validity at \(a_w=0\) is not evidence of physical applicability there.
